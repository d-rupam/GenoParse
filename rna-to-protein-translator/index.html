Triplet Codon Mapping

The parser automatically divides your mRNA sequence into triplet codons (e.g., AUG, GCA) and accurately maps them to their corresponding amino acids.

Stop Codon Detection

Upon detecting a standard stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA), the parser accurately halts sequence synthesis to mirror biological translation termination.

How to Translate RNA to Protein

Our compiler handles raw biological strings seamlessly. Follow these steps:

  • Input the Sequence: Paste your mRNA text or full FASTA record into the left panel. Alternatively, drag and drop a `.fasta` file into the upload zone.
  • Automatic Sanitization: If you paste a FASTA file, the engine will automatically strip headers, spaces, and numbers, isolating only valid RNA bases (A, U, G, C).
  • Execute Translation: Click "Translate to Protein". The logic instantly reads the triplets from 5' to 3' and builds the amino acid chain.
  • Export: Use the "Copy Text" button to copy the raw polypeptide chain, or click "Download FASTA" to export a formatted sequence.

The Compiling Metaphor

In software engineering, "compiling" translates raw source code into functional, executable machine instructions. GenoParse directly fuses this computer science architecture with nucleotide biology. Instead of relying on a backend server, the translation engine runs locally, treating your mRNA transcript as the raw source code and compiling it directly into an active protein.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my genetic sequences uploaded to a server?

No. The translation processes entirely inside your web browser using local javascript algorithms. Your sequence data never leaves your device.

Does it recognize start and stop codons?

Yes. The algorithm maps triplet codons according to standard genetic coding tables and will halt parsing when it encounters recognized stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA).

Does this tool support FASTA files with headers?

Yes. The parser automatically detects and strips the header lines, processing only the raw sequence nucleotides.
