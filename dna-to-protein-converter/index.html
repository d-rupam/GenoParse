How to Convert DNA to Protein

Our compiler handles raw biological strings seamlessly. Follow these steps:

  • Input the Sequence: Paste your DNA Coding Strand text or full FASTA record into the left panel.
  • Automatic Sanitization: The engine automatically strips headers, spaces, and numbers, isolating only valid DNA bases (A, T, G, C).
  • Execute Translation: Click "Compile to Protein". The logic instantly reads the triplets from 5' to 3', bypasses the RNA rendering, and builds the amino acid chain.
  • Export: Use the "Copy Text" button to copy the raw polypeptide chain, or click "Download FASTA" to export a formatted sequence.

The Compiling Metaphor

In software engineering, "compiling" translates raw source code into functional, executable machine instructions. GenoParse directly fuses this computer science architecture with nucleotide biology. Instead of relying on a backend server, the translation engine runs locally, bypassing standard intermediate display files to deliver the final protein.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my genetic sequences uploaded to a server?

No. The compilation processes entirely inside your web browser using local javascript algorithms. Your sequence data never leaves your device.

Does it recognize start and stop codons?

Yes. The algorithm maps triplet DNA codons (e.g., ATG for Start) and will halt parsing when it encounters recognized DNA stop codons (TAA, TAG, TGA).

What happens to Thymine (T)?

Because this tool bypasses the visual RNA step, the background algorithm computes T as U inherently, seamlessly matching DNA coding triplets directly to their respective amino acids.
