About the Developer

GenoParse was conceptualized and developed by Rupam Das, an undergraduate Microbiology researcher blending wet-lab biology with client-side web infrastructure. This tool was engineered to solve the latency and data-privacy issues found in traditional server-based bioinformatics utilities.

How to Execute the Master Pipeline

  • Input the DNA: Paste your DNA Coding Strand or full FASTA record into the left panel. Alternatively, drag and drop a `.fasta` file.
  • Sanitization: The engine will automatically strip headers and spaces, isolating only valid DNA bases.
  • Execute: Click "Run Pipeline". The logic instantly populates both the transcribed RNA panel and the translated Protein panel simultaneously.
  • Export: Use the dedicated Copy and Download buttons to extract either the RNA intermediate or the final amino acid chain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my genetic sequences uploaded to a server?

No. The entire conversion process occurs locally in your web browser. Your sequence data never leaves your device.

What do the asterisks (*) mean in the protein output?

Asterisks (*) represent Stop Codons (UAA, UAG, or UGA). Instead of halting the translation completely, the algorithm uses an asterisk to mark the termination site of a protein chain and continues reading the sequence.

How does the tool separate multiple proteins in a long genome?

For large FASTA files containing multiple genes, the output uses the asterisks (*) as visual dividers. Every continuous block of text between two asterisks represents a distinct polypeptide chain or Open Reading Frame (ORF) mapped from the genome.

Does the tool automatically detect start codons?

Yes. The translation algorithm scans for the first standard start codon (AUG). If found, synthesis begins there. If no start codon is present, it defaults to translating from the very first base (Frame 1).
